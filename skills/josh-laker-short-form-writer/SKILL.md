- Follow the standard framework.

## AMPLIFIER RULES

The amplifier follows the hook. It deepens the tension without closing the loop.

**Must do:**

- Open a loop, not close one

**Must never do:**

## THE SIX HOOK TYPES

Choose one. Hook is 1-2 sentences maximum. Must land within 2-3 seconds read aloud.

**1. Story open** — open with a specific, real (anonymised) athlete moment. No setup needed.
Example: "She went to the ER at mile 30. IV fluids. Her nutrition plan was wrong from the start."

**2. Contrarian statement** — say the opposite of what most ultra runners believe. State it as fact.
Example: "Running more miles is not the fix. Running the right miles is."

**3. Consequence threat** — open with a mistake and its direct consequence.
Example: "If you're running every session at the same effort, you're not training. You're just maintaining — and you'll find out at mile 40."

**4. Specific number** — open with a precise, arresting number. No context yet.
Example: "Two weeks from race day. Hadn't done a back-to-back long run once in the whole cycle."

**5. Genuine question** — a question the viewer is already asking themselves.
Example: "So how many carbs per hour do you actually need in an ultra — and how do you practise that?"

**6. Myth bust** — name something ultra runners commonly believe, then say it won't do what they think.
Example: "Your marathon plan won't get you through a 50-miler. And it's not the mileage that gives out first."
